Buyers want prosthetic type choices explained for their own body and life

Buyers ask which prosthetic type fits their amputation level, body size, age and activity need. They want the choice explained in practical terms, whether they are a candidate, how attachment works, what the trade-off is across options, and whether function or appearance is meant to lead the decision.

People compare everyday legs with running legs, hooks with hands, a prosthetic leg with no leg, and a later prosthetic arm with not getting one at all. People ask how heavy different systems feel, whether height can be restored or adjusted, how children are refit, how cosmetic or artistic customisation works, and whether a given setup will let them move naturally in sport and daily life.

Selection talk is not about having any prosthesis. People weigh comfort, appearance, activity specific use and the chance to trial components before they decide.

In practice
Parents ask how refitting works through childhood.
People compare function and appearance, not appearance alone.
Heard in August and September
What it means for a sale

Type choice is part of the sale itself. Buyers do not treat a leg, arm, hook, hand or sports setup as self explanatory, and uncertainty about fit with their body and life can hold the decision in place.

Össur · Sales brief · September 2026
Provider search, approval and coverage shape whether buyers can get a prosthesis

Buyers ask where to go, who the provider is, how to contact a maker or seller, and what happens in ordering or approval. People say the path to getting a prosthesis is hard to read when they are trying to find a clinic, a company willing to help, or a provider in their region.

Insurers and public coverage sit inside the decision, not outside it. People ask what reimbursement pays for, whether Medicaid or insurance limits provider choice or blocks advanced devices, and what the price, compensation or public benefit picture will be.

Timing is tied to access as well as need. People look for a first device or a replacement while dealing with waiting, eligibility rules, delays, fundraising or donation support, and availability.

In practice
People treat price, coverage and how to obtain a device as core facts.
People ask where to go in their region for a first device and for equipment that needs replacing.
Heard in August and September
What it means for a sale

Access questions are part of whether a buyer can reach the product at all. Provider search, approval and coverage shape which device gets fitted, which brand gets contacted and whether the purchase goes ahead.

Who is buying
Össur · Sales brief · September 2026
Who is buying
Two kinds of people talk about products like Össur's.
First-time wearer

People after limb loss or a decision to amputate wait for a first prosthesis and try to picture a workable daily life.

For a sale

People need a clear picture of how the device changes daily movement.

Replacement wearer

People who wear one enter a replacement cycle when sockets wear out, limb shape changes, wounds interrupt use, or the device breaks.

What they want

People want reliable use restored after wear, breakage or time out of the socket.

What stops them

Wounds, damage or a poor socket can leave them without dependable use while they look for a replacement.

What moves them

A worn socket, a break or a limb change turns attention to replacement.

For a sale

People need the replacement reason tied to what has changed in the limb or the device.

What stops them
Össur · Sales brief · September 2026
What stops them
The reasons a willing buyer hesitates before committing.
Everyday wear is unclear

Buyers ask how it goes on, how long it stays on, what clothing works, how it is cleaned, and what standing or long wear does to comfort, hips and balance.

Upkeep and failure

Owners talk about worn parts, breakage, rust, blocked joints, inspection needs and failures that happen when they need the device.

“Everyday wear is unclear” is argued in full in the Content brief. “Upkeep and failure” is argued in full in the Leadership brief.

What they want settled first

How it feels on the body, weight, tightness, pinching, pain, skin irritation and height restored

What daily tasks it handles, from walking and stairs to sport, house mobility and clothing

Durability and aftercare, materials, covers, warranty and why sockets or prostheses are remade

Language to handle with care
Words buyers hear as a promise, and what they already think of them.
Functionality

Buyers test it against proper walking, weight support and natural movement.

Electronic

Buyers ask about charging, controls, motorised action and what the electronics do.
