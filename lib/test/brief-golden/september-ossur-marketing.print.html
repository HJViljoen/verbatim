The finding
Össur · Marketing brief · September 2026
People do not treat prosthetic mechanics as obvious, they ask what each part does

Viewers ask prosthetics to be explained part by part. People want to know what the foot, knee, ankle, socket and hidden structural parts do, why the device is built that way, and whether a visible part is hydraulic or electronic.

Buyers ask how movement is controlled. People ask whether a prosthesis bears weight, supports walking, bends on command, or is partly cosmetic, and whether hands, hooks, fingers and legs move naturally through electronics, motors or another system. People also ask about charging, controls, sensation, and whether parts can be assembled or changed.

In practice
People ask for the named foot, knee and socket, not only the prosthesis as a whole.
People treat charging and manual controls as basic product facts.
Heard in July, August and September
What it means for the message

Product talk meets basic questions about the mechanism, not only interest in the outcome. The market listens for an explanation of the parts, the control and the reason for the design, and treats those details as part of understanding the product.

People describe the problem as living with a device on the body through putting it on, joint movement, clothing, bathroom use, pain, skin irritation and worn parts.

What makes them stop and argue
They stop on

Comments react to body-matching shape, polished finishes, colour, sparkle, tattoos and natural movement.

They argue over

Comments argue over brands and systems, why someone uses two prosthetic types, anatomical layout, added joints and ankle repositioning.
