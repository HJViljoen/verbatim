The finding
Össur · Content brief · September 2026
Comments ask to see daily wear, from putting it on to bathroom and water use

People ask for the wearing routine in plain steps. Comments want to see how a prosthesis is put on, what socks or suction do, what accessories are for, how a locking system attaches, how it stays secure, and whether it comes off for sleep or other care.

People ask about living in it through the day. Comments ask about joint movement, showering, bathroom use, water, clothing, stump and liner care, skin irritation, pain, weight, comfort across a full day, phantom sensation, and what wearing one feels like. People also ask how children are fitted, whether children outgrow devices, how wear changes across body types or bilateral use, and why some people choose not to wear a prosthesis.

In practice
Comments treat bathroom, bathing and sleep as ordinary use.
Comments ask about socks, liners, covers and stockings as part of the wearing routine.
Heard in August and September
What it means for what to make

People are not treating wear as obvious once the device is fitted. Basic use, care and removal are part of what they want shown, and posts that stay on finished movement leave ordinary ownership unanswered.

What viewers come for
Össur · Content brief · September 2026
What viewers come for, and what loses them

People praise posts that teach basic prosthetic use and show movement that looks smooth and believable.

What earns a response
Clear teaching

People praise videos that explain care, comfort, mobility and what to expect in use.

Smooth movement

People praise advanced prosthetic technology, natural movement, authentic representation and clean visuals.

What loses them
Misleading demo

People complain when cuts change the prosthetic, when adjustment looks easy, or when security looks unsafe.

What people want shown
Össur · Content brief · September 2026
What people want to be shown
Asked for in the comments, in their own terms.
Movement in real life

People ask to see walking, running, biking and how the user controls the prosthesis, with less polish.

Named parts and tech

People ask which foot, knee and socket are used, and whether the system is hydraulic or electronic.

Choice between types

People ask for the benefits and drawbacks of everyday legs, running legs, hands and other bionic products.

Custom look options

People ask to see colours, covers, sleeves, accessories, natural finishes, and changes to height or style.

“Movement in real life” is also in the Sales brief. “Named parts and tech” is argued in full in the Marketing brief. “Choice between types” and “Custom look options” are argued in full in the Sales brief.

Where the confusion starts
Össur · Content brief · September 2026
Where the confusion starts

People do not come to prosthetics with a clear picture of what they are looking at. Confusion sits in both device choice and the social meaning attached to wearing one.

Hardware gets the attention, while fit, recovery and ordinary prosthetic use stay out of view.

“Choice by looks alone” is argued in full in the Sales brief. “Hidden fit work” is argued in full in the Leadership brief.

Choice by looks alone

People miss that different models, components, hooks, arms and legs are chosen for different needs, not appearance alone.

Hidden fit work

People miss how much socket design, alignment, skin tolerance, maintenance and adaptation decide whether a prosthesis works.

Wear it or hide it

People argue over whether amputees should wear a prosthesis, show it, hide it, or need it for work and social life.

Media picture

People meet prosthetics through decontextualised visuals and media tropes that distort amputee bodies and prosthetic use.
