Pain, fit and skin problems decide whether owners keep wearing a prosthesis

Owners describe wear problems in body terms: pain, skin irritation, sweating, fatigue in rehab, tight or pinching fit, worn parts, and mechanisms that stick, corrode or fail to release cleanly.

Users treat fit, socket design, alignment, skin tolerance, maintenance and adaptation as decisive parts of prosthetic success, even though they are less visible than the hardware. When wearability is wrong, people talk about changing the socket, the suspension or the whole prosthesis, and some stop wearing one or look for a replacement.

In practice
Owners treat socket design and alignment as part of the prosthesis, not separate from it.
People replace the socket, the suspension or the whole prosthesis when comfort breaks down.
Heard in August and September
What it means for the business

Wear time is judged on the body, not only by the hardware. Pain, skin damage and poor fit turn a device from a mobility aid into a product people leave off, replace or attach to a different clinic or brand.

Össur · Leadership brief · September 2026
Ottobock is known for microprocessor knees tied to smooth movement and independence

Above-knee users choose Ottobock microprocessor knees for walking they can depend on and for handling hills and uneven ground with less worry.

People attach Ottobock to high-technology prosthetics, especially microprocessor knees. Comments praise smooth movement, reliability and meaningful upgrades, and name the C-Leg line and other knee models when describing the brand. Users tie that technology to rehabilitation progress, confidence and independence after amputation.

In practice
People connect knee choice with hills and uneven ground, not only level walking.
Comments treat meaningful upgrades as part of what makes a premium knee worth wanting.
Heard in August and September
What it means for the business

Ottobock is the named reference point for knee technology tied to movement and independence. That affects how buyers read knee claims across the category, especially for above-knee mobility.

Össur · Leadership brief · September 2026
The risks, in business terms
Risk 1
Basic use is questioned

People question putting a prosthesis on, keeping it on, charging it and controlling it, which puts product claims under pressure.

Risk 2
Other options stay live

People compare hands with hooks, modern mechanical options and no prosthesis, so choice stays open after a device is shown.
